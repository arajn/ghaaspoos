## Historical Parallel: The Railroad Barons of the 1860s

This playbook has been executed before, with devastating effectiveness.

In the 1860s, while prospectors rushed to California chasing gold, the real fortunes were made by those who controlled the railroads. **Leland Stanford, Mark Hopkins, and their associates didn't mine gold — they collected fees on every ounce that moved.** By 1880, they had accumulated more wealth than the miners themselves.

Today's institutional investors are applying the same logic to AI infrastructure. **Microsoft, Google, and Amazon are the miners, desperately seeking computational power.** Blackstone, KKR, and Apollo are building the railroads — and planning to charge rent forever.

The economics are even more favorable than the railroad era. **Once a data center is operational, switching costs for cloud providers are enormous.** Moving terabytes of data and reconfiguring network topologies can cost millions and take months. This creates natural monopolies that didn't exist in the railroad age.
