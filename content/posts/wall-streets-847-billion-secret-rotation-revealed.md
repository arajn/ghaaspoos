## The Historical Playbook

This pattern isn't new. It's the same rotation that crushed growth investors in 1973-1974 and again in 2000-2002.

In 1973, just as today, technology stocks had reached extreme valuations while inflation and interest rates began climbing. The "Nifty Fifty" — blue-chip growth stocks like IBM, Xerox, and Polaroid — collapsed between 65% and 90% as investors rotated into energy, materials, and financials. Value outperformed growth by 40 percentage points over the following three years.

Warren Buffett, who had shuttered his partnership in 1969 citing overvalued markets, began buying aggressively in 1973. His purchases of Washington Post, GEICO, and other value plays during this period generated some of Berkshire Hathaway's best long-term returns.

**The parallel is striking.** Today's institutional rotation exhibits identical characteristics: growth stocks trading at extreme multiples, rising real interest rates, and geopolitical tensions favoring domestic industrial capacity. David Einhorn of Greenlight Capital explicitly referenced this historical precedent in his Q3 investor letter, noting that current conditions mirror "the early stages of the 1970s value renaissance."

The difference? Today's hedge funds have access to real-time positioning data and algorithmic execution that allows for faster, more precise rotations than Buffett could execute with manual processes fifty years ago.

## The Original Framework: The "Regime Change" Indicator

Most analysts focus on P/E ratios or interest rate levels to predict style rotations. They're missing the real driver: **the correlation between bond yields and equity sector performance**.

During ZIRP regimes (2009-2022), growth stocks moved inversely to bond yields — falling rates boosted growth valuations. But in hawkish regimes, this relationship flips. Rising yields begin to correlate positively with value sectors (financials benefit from steeper yield curves) while maintaining negative correlations with growth.

This correlation flip occurred in June 2023, precisely when institutional rotation began accelerating. It's a real-time indicator that most retail investors — and many professionals — don't monitor.

Renaissance Technologies' models likely detected this correlation regime change months before it became obvious in price action. Their allocation shifts weren't contrarian bets; they were systematic responses to statistical relationships breaking down and rebuilding in new patterns.
