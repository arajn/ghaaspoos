Here's the real mechanics: Hedge funds and investment banks borrow yen at Japan's near-zero interest rates (currently 0.1%), immediately convert those yen to dollars or euros, then deploy that capital into higher-yielding assets worldwide. The spread between Japan's rates and global rates becomes pure profit — as long as the yen doesn't strengthen.

**The scale is staggering.** By July 2024, the Bank for International Settlements estimated outstanding yen carry positions at roughly $20 trillion — larger than Japan's entire GDP. Goldman Sachs alone reportedly held $8 billion in short yen positions before the August unwinding.

The beauty of this system isn't the 3-4% annual return. It's the leverage multiplier. Sophisticated players use derivatives to amplify their positions 10:1 or even 20:1, turning a modest rate differential into spectacular returns. Until it all goes wrong.
