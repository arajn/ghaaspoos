## Historical Parallel: The Oil Rush of the 1860s

This playbook has run before. In 1859, Edwin Drake struck oil in Pennsylvania, and within a decade, **John D. Rockefeller had cornered 90% of US oil refining**. The parallels to today's fusion race are striking.

**Vertical integration**: Rockefeller didn't just pump oil — he controlled refineries, pipelines, and distribution. Today's fusion leaders are following the same strategy. Commonwealth Fusion isn't just building reactors; they're developing proprietary superconducting magnets, tritium breeding systems, and power conversion technology.

**First-mover advantages compound**: Rockefeller's early market dominance let him undercut competitors and acquire rivals at fire-sale prices. The first commercial fusion company will have similar leverage — unlimited energy means they can run competitor reactors at a loss while scaling their own operations.

**Regulatory capture**: Rockefeller spent enormous sums lobbying state governments and federal regulators. Today's fusion companies are already embedding former NRC officials and energy department bureaucrats on their advisory boards.

The difference? Rockefeller's oil monopoly was eventually broken up. But fusion technology is far more complex, requiring specialized knowledge and massive capital investments. Natural monopolies are harder to regulate.
